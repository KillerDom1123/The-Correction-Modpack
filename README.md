## Overview

Nobody knows exactly what happened.

The world you inherit is the scarred remnant of something far greater. Abandoned machines still wait for purpose. Ancient knowledge lies scattered across forgotten places. Strange creatures roam lands that should be empty. Those who came before are remembered only as **the Old Civilisation** — some say engineers, some say scholars of magic, some believe they discovered something that neither science nor magic could explain.

Generations have passed since the collapse. Their descendants have begun rebuilding: small settlements take root, forgotten crafts return, lost technologies flicker back to life. But the wilderness is no longer simply *unexplored* — it is **unknown**. Deep beneath the earth and in the dark between the trees, things move that do not behave like anything found in nature.

Before the end, the Old Civilisation reached further than ever before — building machines to travel beyond their world, searching for something. The records stop before the truth is known.

Now you walk their ruins, inheriting their machines, their magic, and their mistakes. The ruins remain. The signals continue. The darkness waits.

**What happened after The Correction?**

## What kind of pack is this?

A slow, dangerous, atmosphere-first experience — **not** a kitchen-sink power fantasy. It blends four things and asks you to earn all of them:

- **Survival** against a hostile world — darkness, hunger, and things that watch.
- **Rebuilding civilisation** from fragments.
- **Earned progression** through technology *and* magic, gated so power feels achieved, not handed out.
- **Exploration** as the heart of the game — the answers are out there, not in your crafting grid.

If you enjoy tension, gradual mastery, and a world that tells its story through what it left behind, this is for you. If you want instant flight and infinite resources by day three, this is not.

---

## Core Features

- **A world that fights back.** Darkness is genuinely dark, harsh biomes and weather bite, and shelter, light, and food are early priorities.
- **Rebuild society.** Grow a colony from a handful of survivors into a living settlement — one that needs walls, guards, and lighting to endure the night.
- **Earn your technology.** A deliberate progression curve — from simple mechanical automation, through industrial power and advanced processing, to digital storage and autocrafting. Each tier is a real project, not a quick recipe.
- **A parallel path of magic.** Rituals, spirits, glyphs, and arcane research stand alongside the machines as a different way of understanding reality.
- **Rare, psychological horror.** The frights here are uncommon by design — dread and the unexpected, not nightly swarms. A single encounter should be a story you tell later.
- **Exploration-driven.** Structures, ruins, and rare biomes hide the world's history; rail networks and waystones connect what you rebuild.
- **A distant final chapter.** Beyond the world lies the Old Civilisation's unfinished journey — reachable only once you've truly rebuilt what they had.

## The Four Paths

The mystery can be approached from any direction. Every major system exists for a narrative reason:

| Path | Question it asks | Mods |
|---|---|---|
| **Engineering** | *What did they build?* | Create (+ addons), Immersive Engineering, PneumaticCraft, Mekanism |
| **Arcane Studies** | *What did they understand?* | Ars Nouveau, Occultism, Hexerei, Forbidden Arcanus, Iron's Spells |
| **The Archive** | *What did they hide?* | Applied Energistics 2, CC:Tweaked |
| **Horror Investigation** | *What survived?* | The horror suite — encountered, never "farmed" |
| **The Descendants** | *Can it be rebuilt?* | MineColonies |
| **The Final Journey** | *Where did they go?* | Ad Astra |

Each path is independently playable. You don't have to follow all of them — but the more you explore, the more the world reveals.

## What to Expect

- **Progression is intentional and slow.** Ore multiplication, autocrafting, flight, and space are gated behind real infrastructure. You will rebuild civilisation the long way.
- **Resources and rewards are scarce.** Exploration is rewarded with *knowledge and progress* more than with overpowered gear.
- **The quest book is an investigation framework, not a checklist.** It offers direction and teaches the mods when you ask "what now?" — but you can ignore half of it and still play your own way.
- **Minimal hand-holding.** The world expects you to leave your base, take risks, and piece things together yourself.

## Getting Started

No spoilers — just footing:

1. **Survive the first nights.** Prioritise shelter, light, and food before anything ambitious. The dark is a threat in its own right.
2. **Don't rush the tech tree.** Early machines and a stable base beat sprinting toward late-game gear you can't sustain.
3. **Leave home.** The pack rewards exploration — ruins and structures are where the world opens up.
4. **Use the quest book for direction, not obligation.** It points you at the major systems when you're unsure what to do next.
5. **Pay attention.** The world documents itself. What you find matters more than it first appears.

## Multiplayer / Dedicated Server

The pack is **server-ready**. To host:

The live server runs on a **Proxmox VM** (Forge 47.4.10) and is deployed over FTP. Its mod list is in **[docs/SERVER_MODS.md](docs/SERVER_MODS.md)**. `Servers/Server v2` (built 2026-11-02) is now a local test bed.

1. Install a matching **Forge 1.20.1 (47.4.10)** dedicated server with the **same mod list and versions** as clients.
2. Sync the mods with `python scripts/sync_server_mods.py`.
   - It targets the VM by default, with the FTP login from `~/.spooky_ftp_netrc`; `--server <folder>` targets a local server folder instead.
   - It copies the client's mods minus the client-only list and takes anything else out of `mods/`, moving it aside rather than deleting it.
   - It shows the changes and asks before touching the VM. `--dry-run` only shows them.
3. Copy **`config/`** (includes the OpenLoader datapacks and quests), **`defaultconfigs/`**, and **`kubejs/`**. Copy `saves/<world>` → the server's `world/` if migrating an existing map.
4. Keep `mods/` and `kubejs/startup_scripts/` **identical** on server and clients (registry parity), or players will be unable to connect. Then restart the server and regenerate the server mod list with `python tools/generate_server_modlist.py`; it reads the VM and reports parity.

Client-only tweaks (audio, video, personal options) stay on each player's client and are ignored by the server.

## Custom commands

These are all the commands the pack adds itself; the mods bring their own. "Operator" means permission level 2. The server
console counts as an operator and acts in the overworld.

| Command | Arguments | Who | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| `/sanity` | none | any player | Shows your own sanity: `<name> — sanity 38.4/100  [Dread]`. Read-only. From the console, give a target. |
| `/sanity <target>` | `target`: one player, by name or a selector that matches exactly one (`@p`, `@s`) | operator | The same for another player. |
| `/tickfixes stats` | none | operator | One status line for `spooky_tick_fixes`: the activation range, portal and moving-block counts for your dimension, average tick time, horror ray-cast counters, chunk-guard counters (including off-thread chunk requests and their top callers), and chunk-safe ticking counters (barrier reads and their callers, disk and memory loads). Counts run from server start. |
| `/tickfixes probe <x> <z>` | `x`, `z`: whole-number block coordinates in your dimension (y is always 64) | operator | At the start of your dimension's next tick, reads that block the way ticking code would. Replies with the block, how it was answered (loaded / from memory / from disk / barrier, with the reason) and how long it took. Probing ungenerated terrain doesn't generate it. |
| `/tickfixes wendigo` | none | operator | Lists every loaded Wendigo: where it is, how far from you, and whether it's calm, keeping away from a player, provoked (by whom and how) or retreating after calming down. The first line gives the patch's on/off state and the provoke, calm-down and vanish counts since server start. |
| `/pregen` or `/pregen status` | none | operator | `spooky_pregen`'s state: enabled, players, how long the server has been empty, hold, guards, free disk, the current Chunky task and its progress, the current blob and why it was picked, anchors, and each job's next radius. |
| `/pregen next` | none | operator | What it would generate next and why. Nothing is generated, but if no blob is chosen yet it picks one and keeps it. |
| `/pregen start` | none | operator | **Force-start**: begins (or resumes) work now, without waiting for an empty server, alongside any players already online. It clears a hold. The next player to join pauses it and ends force mode, as do `/pregen stop`, `/pregen hold` and a restart. |
| `/pregen stop` | none | operator | Ends force mode: back to running only while the server is empty (it pauses at once if players are on). |
| `/pregen hold` | none | operator | Stops all pre-generation (pausing the current task, and ending force mode) until `/pregen resume`. It persists across restarts. |
| `/pregen resume` | none | operator | Allows work again. It restarts once the server has been empty for the grace time. |
| `/pregen skip` | none | operator | Cancels the current task and moves on. A job or blob goes to its next radius; an edge square counts as done. |
| `/pregen reload` | none | operator | Re-reads `config/spooky_pregen.json` (no restart needed). |
| `/pregen radius` | none | operator | Shows the blob size: the largest radius a blob grows to (the square is twice that across), and whether it comes from the config or the command. |
| `/pregen radius <blocks>` | `blocks`: a whole number from 16 to 8192 | operator | Sets the blob size at once, for the blob in progress too. If that blob is already larger, its running square is cancelled (what it made stays) and a new blob is picked. It's kept across restarts. Example: `/pregen radius 256` for 512 × 512-block blobs. |
| `/pregen radius reset` | none | operator | Goes back to the config's `blobMaxRadius` (512 in the pack). |

Details: `docs/sanity_mechanic.md` §15.5, `tools/spooky_tick_fixes/README.md` (Commands) and `tools/spooky_pregen/README.md`
(Commands). The pack's datapack functions (`wa_buff:tick` for the Weeping Angels buff, and the Incendium clock overrides)
run on their own from tick tags and aren't meant to be run by hand.

## Repository & Configuration

This repository tracks the pack's **configuration and custom content**, not the mod binaries:

- **Tracked:** `config/` (all mod configs, OpenLoader datapacks, FTB Quests), `kubejs/` (custom scripts/items), `defaultconfigs/` (per-world defaults), the `docs/` markdown, and `tools/` (the modlist generators and the source of the pack's own mods, listed under **Local Fixes** in the modlist).
- **Building:** each `tools/<mod>/build.py` uses the shared `tools/_javabuild.py` (JDK 17 plus the CurseForge install's libraries; no Gradle). The version lives in the mod's `mods.toml` and `MANIFEST.MF`. `--install` copies the jar into `mods/` and `local/` and removes older versions there.
- **Built here, now disabled:** `immptl_dimthread_compat` — a one-mixin mod that let Immersive Portals and DimThreads run together. DimThreads was disabled on 2026-11-06: its 1.2.1 ticks every dimension under one global lock, so it gave no parallelism. Both jars are `.jar.disabled` on clients and the server; the source stays in `tools/immptl_dimthread_compat/` in case DimThreads returns.
- **Built here:** `spooky_tick_fixes` — behaviour-preserving server-tick fixes (dormant mimics, TOWW follow range, The Obsessed sync, Whispering Spirits, Fragile Torches, Farm & Charm, MCreator animation handlers, Ad Astra Extra, Full Immersion, Alex's Caves, Citadel, TOWW's per-level sync, Antlers' day counter, and guards that stop the horror mods' look ray casts, The Obsessed's block reads and vanilla villagers' bed check from loading chunks on the main thread), plus an adaptive **activation range** (far mobs tick less often while the server is behind; MineColonies, guards and scripted horror are exempt; `/tickfixes stats` shows its status). Since 1.4.0 it also reworks Antlers' **Wendigo**, a deliberate gameplay change: it keeps away from players, attacks only a player who provokes it (a hit, coming too close, or staring it down), retreats and vanishes after calming down, and despawns like other monsters (`[antlersWendigo]`, `/tickfixes wendigo`). Its loot comes from the `WendigoTrophies` datapack. Each patch has a switch in `config/spooky_tick_fixes-common.toml`. Build it with `python tools/spooky_tick_fixes/build.py --install`. It ships on clients and the server, but the server can run it alone. Details: `tools/spooky_tick_fixes/README.md` and `docs/SERVER_TICK_AUDIT_2026-11-06.md`.
- **Built here:** `spooky_pregen` — pre-generates the world with Chunky while the dedicated server is empty and pauses the moment a player logs in. It works through any fixed points in `config/spooky_pregen.json` (the pack lists none), then grows "blobs": squares on the edge of the generated area nearest to where players are (their last logout positions and beds), 256 → 512 blocks in radius by default. It stops while free disk space is under 2 GB; the optional daily growth cap is off in the pack. `/pregen status|next|start|stop|hold|resume|skip|reload|radius`. Inert on clients and in singleplayer. Details: `tools/spooky_pregen/README.md`.
- **Built here:** `farsight_immptl_compat` — restores Farsight's client chunk keeping under Immersive Portals. Client-only; the sync never copies it to the server.
- **Pregen map:** `python tools/pregen_map.py` reads the VM over FTP (read-only) and writes `tools/build/pregen_map.html`: what is generated, the selected blob, the square generating now and what is still to come, over a faint terrain relief. `python tools/pregen_map.py --serve` keeps it live instead: a page on `http://localhost:8765` (this PC only) that re-reads the VM every 10 s (`--interval`) and repaints in place, keeping your zoom and position. Only what changed on the VM is downloaded after the first read.
- **Not tracked:** `mods/`, `saves/`, caches, logs, and machine-specific files (see `.gitignore`).

The pack is heavily **rebalanced by design** — see **[docs/CONFIG_CHANGES.md](docs/CONFIG_CHANGES.md)** for a full per-mod record of every configuration change and the reasoning behind it (progression gating, horror rarity, colony danger, etc.).

Because `mods/` isn't tracked, use **[modlist.md](modlist.md)** as the authoritative record of which mods and versions the configuration targets.
